# 10.2 Distribution-Based Distillation {#sec-vsd}

Several works have pursued this distribution-based distillation concurrently under different names, including Distributional Matching Distillation (DMD) ([Yin et al., 2024](references.md#ref-yin2024one); [Yin et al., 2024](references.md#ref-yin2024improved)), Variational Score Distillation (VSD) ([Poole et al., 2023](references.md#ref-pooledreamfusion); [Wang et al., 2023](references.md#ref-wang2023prolificdreamer); [Luo et al., 2023](references.md#ref-luo2023diff); [Lu and Song, 2024](references.md#ref-lu2024simplifying)), and Score Identity Distillation (SiD) ([Zhou et al., 2024](references.md#ref-zhou2024score)). Despite technical differences, they share the same principle: train a generator whose forward-noised marginals match those of the teacher. We focus on VSD as a representative formulation, since the others follow similar principles.[^1]

## 10.2.1 Formulation of VSD as a Representative Approach {#formulation-of-vsd}

**Forward Process.** Let $\{p_t\}_{t \in [0,T]}$ denote the marginal densities of a forward diffusion process induced by

$$
{\mathbf{x}}_t = \alpha_t {\mathbf{x}}_0 + \sigma_t \boldsymbol{\epsilon}, \quad \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0}, {\mathbf{I}}),
$$

with initial distribution $p_0 = p_{\mathrm{data}}$. In contrast, let $p_0^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ denote the distribution of synthetic samples generated by a deterministic one-step generator ${\mathbf{G}}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}({\mathbf{z}})$ from latent variables ${\mathbf{z}} \sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}({\mathbf{z}})$. Define $\{p_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\}_{t \in [0,T]}$ as the marginal densities obtained by applying the same forward diffusion process to $p_0^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$, that is,

<span id="eq-vsd-forward"></span>

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\mathbf{x}}_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}} := \alpha_t {\mathbf{G}}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}({\mathbf{z}}) + \sigma_t \boldsymbol{\epsilon},
\end{aligned}
\tag{10.2.1}
$$

where ${\mathbf{z}} \sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}$ and $\boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0}, {\mathbf{I}})$. Thus, both $p_t$ and $p_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ share the same Gaussian diffusion kernel $p_t({\mathbf{x}}_t| {\mathbf{x}}_0)$ but differ in their starting distributions ($p_{\mathrm{data}}$ vs. $p_0^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ of one-step synthetic samples).

**Training Objective and Gradient.** The literature typically adopts the KL divergence to match the distributions $p_t$ and $p_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$, commonly by minimizing

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{VSD}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) :=\mathbb{E}_t \left[ \omega(t)  \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}(p_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \, \| \, p_t) \right]
= \mathbb{E}_{t, {\mathbf{z}}, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}} \left[ \omega(t) \left( \log p_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}}_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) - \log p_t({\mathbf{x}}_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) \right) \right],
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\omega(t)\geq 0$ is a time-dependent weighting function. We will discuss in [Section 10.2.3](02-distribution-based-distillation.md#subsec-vsd-discussion) why the KL divergence plays a special role in distribution-level distillation.

As shown in ([Wang et al., 2023](references.md#ref-wang2023prolificdreamer)), at the population level, the global optimum is achieved when $p_0^{\boldsymbol{\theta}^*}=p_{\mathrm{data}}$, indicating that the generator’s distribution matches the data distribution. Thus, the VSD objective provides a principled distribution-matching loss for learning $p_{\mathrm{data}}$.

However, the density-based formulation of the objective lacks an efficient training mechanism. Fortunately, by taking the gradient with respect to $\boldsymbol{\theta}$, we arrive at the expression in [Equation (10.2.2)](02-distribution-based-distillation.md#eq-vsd-grad), which is summarized in the following proposition. For notational simplicity, we denote $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t := {\mathbf{x}}_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ as defined in [Equation (10.2.1)](02-distribution-based-distillation.md#eq-vsd-forward).

<span id="vsd-grad"></span>

**Proposition 10.2.1: $\boldsymbol{\theta}$-Gradient of $\mathcal{L}_{\text{VSD}}$**

We have

<span id="eq-vsd-grad"></span>

$$
\begin{aligned}
        \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{VSD}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \mathbb{E}_{t, {\mathbf{z}}, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}} \left[ \omega(t)\alpha_t  \left(
\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}} \log p_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t) - \nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}} \log p_t(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)
\right) \cdot \partial_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} {\mathbf{G}}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}({\mathbf{z}}) \right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{10.2.2}
$$

**Proof for Proposition.**

The derivation applies the chain rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{VSD}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
=
\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\mathbb{E}}_t
\left[
\omega(t)\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\|p_t)
\right]
\\
&= {\mathbb{E}}_{t,{\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}}\!\left[
\omega(t)\,
\partial_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
\big(\log p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)-\log p_t(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)\big)
\right] \\
&= {\mathbb{E}}_{t,{\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}} \left[
\omega(t)\left(
\underbrace{\partial_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\log p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)}_{\text{first}}
+ (\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t))^\top \partial_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t
- (\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t))^\top \partial_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t
\right)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

The first term vanishes by the score-function identity:

$$
{\mathbb{E}}_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t\sim p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}}\!\left[\partial_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\log p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)\right]
= \int \partial_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}} p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}})  \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}}
= \partial_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}  \int p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}})  \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}}
= \partial_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(1)=0.
$$

Using the reparameterization $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t=\alpha_t{\mathbf{G}}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}({\mathbf{z}})+\sigma_t{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}$ gives $\partial_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t=\alpha_t\partial_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\mathbf{G}}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}({\mathbf{z}})$, hence

$$
\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{VSD}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
= {\mathbb{E}}_{t,{\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}} \left[\omega(t) \alpha_t
\big(\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)-\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)\big)^\top
\partial_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\mathbf{G}}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}({\mathbf{z}})\right].
$$

This proves [Equation (10.2.2)](02-distribution-based-distillation.md#eq-vsd-grad). See [Section D.5](../../appendices/d-supplementary-proofs/index.md#app-sec-flow-map) for details.

We observe that the score functions naturally emerge when taking the gradient with respect to $\boldsymbol{\theta}$. Consequently, we require approximations of the score $\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)$ for the one-step generator and $\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)$ for the data distribution, as will be detailed in the following subsection.

## 10.2.2 Training Pipeline of VSD {#subsec-vsd-training}

Existing works ([Yin et al., 2024](references.md#ref-yin2024one); [Yin et al., 2024](references.md#ref-yin2024improved); [Poole et al., 2023](references.md#ref-pooledreamfusion); [Wang et al., 2023](references.md#ref-wang2023prolificdreamer); [Luo et al., 2023](references.md#ref-luo2023diff); [Lu and Song, 2024](references.md#ref-lu2024simplifying)) typically address this via a bi-level optimization approach: training a new diffusion model on samples from ${\mathbf{G}}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}({\mathbf{z}})$ to approximate $\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}} \log p_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)$, and employing a pre-trained diffusion model to provide a proxy for the intractable oracle score function $\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}} \log p_t(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)$ on synthetic samples $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t$. More precisely, training proceeds by alternating between two phases:

-   **Score Estimation Phase.** Fix $\boldsymbol{\theta}$. Let $\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_0={\mathbf{G}}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}({\mathbf{z}})$ and $\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_t=\alpha_t \hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_0+\sigma_t{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}$ with ${\mathbf{z}}\sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}$, ${\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},{\mathbf{I}})$. Train $s_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}$ by DSM using the known Gaussian diffusion kernel $p_t({\mathbf{x}}_t|{\mathbf{x}}_0)$:

    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{DSM}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta};\boldsymbol{\theta})
        ={\mathbb{E}}_{t,{\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}}\Big\|\,{\mathbf{s}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}(\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_t,t)
        - \nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}_t}\log p_t(\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_t|\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_0)\,\Big\|^2,
    $$

    which yields ${\mathbf{s}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}(\cdot,t)\approx \nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\cdot)$ at optimum (for fixed $\boldsymbol{\theta}$).

-   **Generator Update Phase.** With $s_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}$ frozen (stop-grad), $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ is updated by using the gradient in [Equation (10.2.2)](02-distribution-based-distillation.md#eq-vsd-grad), replacing the individual score terms by their respective proxies:

    $$
    {\mathbf{s}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t,t)\approx \nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t), \,\, \text{and} \,\, {\mathbf{s}}_{\boldsymbol{\phi}^\times}(\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_t,t)\approx \nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)\ \ \text{(teacher)}.
    $$

    [Equation (10.2.2)](02-distribution-based-distillation.md#eq-vsd-grad) then approximately becomes:

    $$
    \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{VSD}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
        \approx \mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{z}},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}\Big[\omega(t)\alpha_t
          \big({\mathbf{s}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}(\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_t,t) - {\mathbf{s}}_{\boldsymbol{\phi}^\times}(\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}_t,t)\big)^{\top}
          \partial_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}{\mathbf{G}}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}({\mathbf{z}})
        \Big].
    $$

These two phases are alternated throughout training. Ideally, the auxiliary model tracks the current student score ${\mathbf{s}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}(\cdot,t)\approx\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\cdot)$, while the pre-trained teacher provides an approximation to the target score ${\mathbf{s}}_{\boldsymbol{\phi}^\times}(\cdot,t)\approx\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t(\cdot)$. At the population level, if these two score functions agree at a noised level with $\sigma_t>0$ and $\alpha_t\neq 0$, then their corresponding normalized densities agree, $p_t^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=p_t$. Since Gaussian noising is injective when $\alpha_t\neq 0$, this further implies $p_0^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=p_{\mathrm{data}}$.

In practice, both scores are represented by finite learned networks, so these equalities characterize the ideal population solution rather than a guarantee of alternating optimization.

## 10.2.3 Beyond KL: General Divergences and Why KL Is Special {#subsec-vsd-discussion}

The KL divergence is not essential to the distribution-matching principle of VSD. In principle, one may use a general $f$-divergence (see [Equation (1.1.4)](../01-deep-generative-modeling/01-what-is-deep-generative-modeling.md#eq-f-div)):

$$
\mathcal{D}_f(p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\|p_t)
=
\int p_t({\mathbf{x}})\,
f\!\left(
\frac{p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}})}{p_t({\mathbf{x}})}
\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} {\mathbf{x}}.
$$

What makes the reverse KL particularly convenient is its gradient structure. Define the density ratio

$$
r_t({\mathbf{x}})
:=
\frac{p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}})}{p_t({\mathbf{x}})}.
$$

For a twice-differentiable convex $f$ and positive densities, the pathwise gradient can be written as

$$
\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\mathcal{D}_f(p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\|p_t)
=
{\mathbb{E}}_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t\sim p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}}
\left[
r_t(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)f''\!\big(r_t(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)\big)
\big(
\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)
-
\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t)
\big)^\top
\partial_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t
\right].
$$

Conceptually, the gradient has three components:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\mathcal{D}_f
=
{\mathbb{E}}\Big[
\underbrace{r_t f''(r_t)}_{\text{density-ratio weighting}}
\times
\underbrace{
\big(
\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
-
\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t
\big)^\top
}_{\text{score mismatch}}
\times
\underbrace{
\partial_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t
}_{\text{parameter sensitivity}}
\Big].
\end{aligned}
$$

The first term determines *where and how strongly* to update, the second gives the score-mismatch direction, and the last maps this sample-space signal back to the generator parameters.

This expression reveals a useful common structure: all such $f$-divergences use the same *score-mismatch direction*,

$$
\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}({\mathbf{x}})
-
\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log p_t({\mathbf{x}})
=
\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log r_t({\mathbf{x}}),
$$

while the choice of divergence changes how strongly different regions are weighted through $r_t f''(r_t)$. Thus, for the usual strictly convex choices of $f$, the population distribution-matching condition remains $p_t^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}=p_t$, while the choice of divergence changes how different density-ratio regimes are weighted during optimization through $r_t f''(r_t)$.

For the reverse KL used in VSD, $f(r)=r\log r$, and therefore

$$
r f''(r)=1.
$$

The density-ratio weighting disappears, recovering the score-difference gradient in [Equation (10.2.2)](02-distribution-based-distillation.md#eq-vsd-grad). Consequently, once the student and target scores are available, reverse-KL training does not require an additional pointwise density-ratio estimator. By contrast, a general $f$-divergence requires the factor $r_t f''(r_t)$; although the score difference provides $\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}}\log r_t$, the ratio $r_t$ itself is not directly available for an implicit generator and generally requires additional estimation.

For example, the forward KL corresponds to $f(r)=-\log r$, for which $r f''(r)=1/r$. It therefore places greater weight on regions where the model density is small relative to the target density. This illustrates how different divergences can share the same underlying score-mismatch direction while emphasizing different density-ratio regimes.

Finally, this score-mismatch view connects directly to the Wasserstein gradient-flow perspective in [Section B.3](../../appendices/b-density-evolution/index.md#sec-wgf-distribution-level-training). At the distribution level, reverse-KL gradient flow moves particles according to the target score minus the model score. The VSD parameter gradient contains the same score mismatch, pulled back through the generator Jacobian $\partial_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t$. Thus, the Wasserstein view describes the ideal distribution-space motion, while VSD realizes this motion through updates of a finite-dimensional shared generator.

[^1]: Historically, VSD ([Wang et al., 2023](references.md#ref-wang2023prolificdreamer)) was introduced for text-to-3D generation, where a pre-trained 2D diffusion model provides distributional supervision through a differentiable renderer. Its single-particle (Dirac) specialization recovers the basic SDS ([Poole et al., 2023](references.md#ref-pooledreamfusion)) update, up to the corresponding time-weighting and prediction-parameterization conventions.
